CMHC's Apartment Construction Loan Program (ACLP), Explained

The ACLP is CMHC's direct, low-cost lending program for rental construction. It covers the riskiest part of a project, from first shovel to stabilized rents, and it now runs on a points scorecard.

The Apartment Construction Loan Program (ACLP), formerly known as the Rental Construction Financing Initiative, is CMHC's own lending program for new purpose-built rentals. Unlike mortgage loan insurance, CMHC is the lender. It provides low-cost funding during construction through to stabilized operations, with CMHC mortgage loan insurance built in.

Who and what qualifies

  • Projects: construction of standard rental housing, or conversion of non-residential space to rental. At least five fully self-contained units, primarily residential, for long-term stays.
  • Non-residential space: no more than 30% of gross floor area and 30% of total cost.
  • Borrowers: private builders and developers, non-profits, rental co-operatives and other levels of government.
  • Loan size: a minimum of $1 million.
  • Experience: at least five years operating similar rental property (or a long-term contract with an experienced third-party property manager), and a completed similar project on time and on budget (or a fixed-price contract with an experienced general contractor).
  • Net worth: at least 25% of the loan amount, with a minimum of $100,000.

The affordability requirement

Every ACLP project must meet one of two affordability criteria for at least 10 years:

  • Criteria A: at least 20% of units with rents at or below 30% of the median total income of all families in the market (Statistics Canada).
  • Criteria B: rents set under a federal, provincial, territorial or municipal affordable housing program that approves and supports the project.

The points scorecard

The ACLP uses a points system. Stronger commitments earn more favourable lending terms. Points come from:

  • Affordability depth and share: for example, 20% of units at 30% of median income for 10 years earns 0 points. 30% of units at 15% of median income for 20+ years earns 65.
  • Market adjustment: 0, 9 or 18 points depending on how hard affordability is to achieve in a given market.
  • Climate commitment: energy performance against the 2020 codes. Low-rise Part 9 buildings earn 10 points at 20% better than code, 20 at 40% and 35 at 70%. Part 3 buildings score against NECB tiers from 10 to 35 points.

Other key terms from CMHC's highlight sheet:

  • A fixed rate locked in at the first advance. Interest-only payments are financed by the loan during construction. The borrower pays interest only from occupancy until 12 consecutive months of stabilized income, then principal and interest to the end of the term.
  • Minimum debt coverage ratio of 1.10 on the residential component, qualified at the higher of 2.00% or CMHC's indicative rate plus 1.00%.
  • No mandatory rental-achievement holdback and no mandatory replacement reserve.
  • The insurance premium is not passed on to the borrower (PST on the premium may apply).
  • Application fee of $200 per unit for the first 100 units.

ACLP vs. MLI Select

The two are often confused. MLI Select is insurance on a loan from a private approved lender. You can use it for construction financing or as the long-term take-out. ACLP is a CMHC loan for construction through stabilization, with its own scorecard and stricter borrower requirements. Projects that don't fit ACLP's timelines or funding availability often use conventional construction financing, then an MLI Select take-out.

Is it right for your project?

ACLP terms are attractive, but the program is competitive, prioritizes projects with stronger social outcomes, and has a detailed application with fixed timelines. For smaller Ottawa projects the minimum loan, experience requirements and documentation can decide the question before the rates do. We look at both routes early, so the design supports whichever one you pursue. We prepare your financing package and work with your lender or broker to secure it.

Questions

What is the minimum loan under the ACLP?

CMHC's minimum ACLP loan is $1 million, for purpose-built rental projects with at least five self-contained units.

Can the ACLP fund 100% of construction costs?

Projects scoring at least 65 points on the ACLP scorecard can qualify for up to 100% loan-to-cost on the residential component, with up to 50-year amortization.

Is ACLP the same as MLI Select?

No. ACLP is a CMHC loan for construction through stabilization. MLI Select is CMHC mortgage loan insurance on a loan from an approved private lender, often used for construction or take-out financing.
