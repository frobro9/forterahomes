Triplex, Fourplex or Sixplex? Why Five Units Changes Your Financing in Ottawa

Most owners start by asking how many units the zoning allows. The better question is how many units the financing rewards. In Canada, that line sits at five.

Ontario's housing legislation and Ottawa's zoning now allow more homes on many residential lots than they did a few years ago. Ontario's More Homes Built Faster Act (Bill 23) permits up to three units on most serviced urban residential lots, and many Ottawa zones allow more. That has made triplexes and fourplexes a common conversation for Ottawa landowners.

But from a financing point of view, there is a bigger step between four units and five than between any other two numbers.

The five-unit line

CMHC's two main tools for rental construction both start at five units:

  • CMHC MLI Select, the points-based mortgage loan insurance product for rental buildings, requires a minimum of five units.
  • The Apartment Construction Loan Program (ACLP), CMHC's low-cost construction loan, requires purpose-built rental projects of at least five units and a minimum loan of $1 million.

Above that line, a well-planned building can qualify for up to 95% loan-to-cost, amortization of 40 to 50 years, and lower insurance premiums. Below it, you're generally working with conventional construction and term financing, which usually means more of your own equity in the project and shorter amortizations.

Why amortization matters so much

Rental lenders size a loan by the building's income. They look at the debt coverage ratio: net operating income divided by annual mortgage payments. MLI Select requires a minimum of 1.10 for standard rental housing.

A longer amortization lowers the annual payment, so the same income supports a larger loan. On the same rents, the difference between a 25-year and a 50-year amortization can be hundreds of thousands of dollars of borrowing capacity on a small building. That's money that doesn't have to come out of your pocket. We walk through the math in our DCR explainer.

The fifth and sixth units also spread fixed costs (land, servicing, design, permits) across more doors, which often lowers the cost per unit.

Small buildings and the points system

Because MLI Select's affordability thresholds are percentages of total units, small buildings reach them quickly. On a six-unit building, one affordable unit is about 17% of the units. That clears the 15% threshold for 70 points on its own. Two affordable units clear 25% for 100 points. See how to score 50, 70 or 100 points for the full breakdown.

When a fourplex is still the right answer

Five units isn't right for every lot. Zoning, lot size, servicing capacity, parking, height limits and the cost of a Committee of Adjustment application can all make a triplex or fourplex the better project. Part 3 construction requirements can also add cost to larger buildings. The point is to compare the options with real numbers before you commit to a design.

How we approach it

When an owner brings us a lot, we look at what the zoning allows, what the site can physically hold and what each option costs to build. Then we look at how each option finances. Sometimes the answer is a fourplex. Often the five- or six-unit version leaves the owner with less cash in the deal and a stronger building. We prepare your financing package and work with your lender or broker to secure it.

Questions

Can a fourplex use CMHC MLI Select?

No. MLI Select requires a minimum of five units (50 units or beds for retirement homes).

Does the ACLP fund small buildings?

The ACLP requires purpose-built rental projects with at least five self-contained units and a minimum loan of $1 million.

How many units can I build on my lot in Ottawa?

It depends on the zone, lot size, servicing and height and setback rules. A zoning and feasibility review answers this before you spend on design.
