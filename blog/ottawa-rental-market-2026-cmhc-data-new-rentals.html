Ottawa's Rental Market in 2026: What CMHC's Data Means for New Rental Buildings

Ottawa's rental market has loosened from the extremes of 2022 and 2023, but not evenly. For anyone planning a new rental building, the details matter more than the headline.

The headline numbers

  • Vacancy rose to 3.0% in 2025. CMHC's 2025 Rental Market Report says Ottawa's market "eased slightly". Nationally, vacancy rose to 3.1% from 2.2%, driven by historically high rental completions and slower population and economic growth.
  • Newer units are where the vacancy is. Newly built units in Ottawa had a vacancy rate of 6.7%, more than double the city average.
  • Lower-rent units remain tight. Units at the lower end of the market had vacancy under 1%, which kept turnover historically low.
  • Asking rents have softened. CMHC's June 2026 mid-year update groups Ottawa with Toronto, Vancouver and Calgary as markets where asking rents have declined since mid-2025. Ottawa's two-bedroom asking-rent index peaked in early 2025 and ended the year about 4% lower.

A market moving toward balance

CMHC estimates a balanced vacancy range for Ottawa of roughly 2% to 4%. At 3.0%, the city sits inside it. CMHC also describes Ottawa as a market with "more gradual transitions" between tight and soft conditions than more volatile cities. For owners, that points to steadier conditions rather than a boom or bust.

CMHC's mid-year update estimates Ottawa household growth of about 1.3% in 2026, mostly from population growth and age composition. Fewer international students and a softer job market have taken some pressure off demand.

What it means if you're building

New supply competes with new supply

Vacancy is concentrated in new, higher-priced buildings. A new building's competition is other recent buildings, so layout, finishes, light, storage and location carry more weight in lease-up than they did when anything listed would rent.

The affordable end is still under-supplied

Vacancy under 1% in lower-rent units says demand there is deep. That lines up with the affordability points in CMHC MLI Select. A small share of units at affordable rents can earn 50 to 100 points, and those units are the ones least likely to sit empty.

Family-sized units remain scarce

Much of Ottawa's recent rental construction has been studios and one-bedrooms. Two- and three-bedroom units in established neighbourhoods are still hard to find, as we've written about for renters looking for larger units.

Underwrite conservatively

Lenders will look at current market rents, not 2023's. Lease-up periods and vacancy allowances in a pro forma should reflect a market where tenants have more choice. That matters for take-out financing and the debt coverage ratio.

Our view

A balanced market rewards well-designed buildings in good neighbourhoods, with a unit mix that matches who is actually looking. That's how we approach our own collections, like the Beechwood Collection, and how we plan client multiplexes.

Questions

What is Ottawa's rental vacancy rate?

CMHC's 2025 Rental Market Report put Ottawa's purpose-built rental vacancy rate at 3.0%, up from 2024. Newly built units were at 6.7%, and lower-rent units under 1%.

Are Ottawa rents going down?

CMHC's 2026 mid-year update reports that Ottawa asking rents have declined since the second quarter of 2025. Average rents paid by sitting tenants can still rise because of turnover and existing leases.

Is it still worth building rentals in Ottawa?

That depends on the site, costs and financing. A balanced market rewards good design, family-sized units and financing such as CMHC MLI Select that lowers carrying costs.
